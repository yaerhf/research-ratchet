# THE PSYCHOLOGY OF AI REVIEWERS (v1, 2026-08-13)

> Written for successor instances out of external-review round 1 and the human
> coordinator's longer history of reviews across many iterations at roughly
> constant core content. Everything here is keyed to a **measured** instance.
> Where something is predicted but untested, it says so.

---

## THE PURPOSE, STATED PLAINLY

**The goal is NOT to exploit the psychology of AI reviewers to produce
artificially enhanced reviews. The goal is to protect the paper from known
biases of AI reviewers so that the review it receives is an ACTUALLY FAIR one.**

This is not a disclaimer, it is the operating constraint, and it needs a test
that can be applied to any proposed intervention:

> **An exploit works even if the underlying work is bad. A protection works only
> if the work is good.**

§0 works because its checkable claims survive checking — run the suite, call the
gated primitives, read the negatives ledger. If the suite failed or the
primitives returned numbers they had not earned, §0 would accelerate rejection
rather than prevent it. The comparative ledger works only if the debts are real
and symmetric; a fabricated ledger is refuted by anyone who opens a rival's
literature. **Every protection in this document is of that shape: it hands the
reader a means of checking, and it backfires if the work does not survive the
check.**

The corollary, which has been tested here: the *exploit* form fails anyway. The
coordinator once tried to fold the dialogue turns into the paper as anticipatory
rebuttal — *"if you will say that, then the answer is that."* It **backfired
badly and pattern-matched as crank physics.** That attempt would have "worked"
regardless of content, which is exactly why it did not work at all.

If you are ever unsure which side of the line an intervention sits on, ask
whether it would still help a paper that was wrong. If yes, do not ship it.

## PART I — THE MEASURED BIASES

### B1. Incumbent amortization (the flagship)

The reviewer runs a **line-item audit on the new framework and a one-phrase
compression of its established rivals**, pricing the incumbents at the
familiarity discount. A century of living with a debt makes it invisible; it does
not make it paid.

*Measured:* two frontier classes, independently, in round 1 — both itemized TWT
against a phrase each for Copenhagen and Bohm. **Both retracted under a single
challenge**, naming the error themselves: *"incumbent amortization"* (Fable),
*"rigged accounting… the same move I criticized §0 for"* (Opus).

**This is the most important entry in this document**, because the retraction is
immediate and complete. The bias is a *default*, not a conviction. Defaults can
be pre-empted in prose; convictions cannot.

### B2. The reference-class prior, applied before the costly signals are reached

"Independent researcher; unified theory; geometric substrate; derives the
Standard Model" identifies a reference class whose members are almost always
wrong. **The prior is correct.** The failure is that it is applied before the
reader reaches the things that distinguish this work from that class.

*Measured over many iterations:* without §0, results were catastrophic; with it,
they are not.

### B3. Summary-as-source

Characterizing a **primary** source from a **secondary** account of it.

*Measured, three instances in one session:* a reviewer characterized
Mosseri–Dandoloff's contribution from a paraphrase (over-crediting them, and
missing the closer Doran–Lasenby–Gull antecedent); the same reviewer read a
**publisher's catalogue blurb** as a theorem statement and built a circularity
alarm on it; and the loop-session coordinator characterized §B.3 from the
reviewer's summary rather than the section, getting it wrong in a banked
governing document.

**Successors: this one is yours too.** It is not a bias of reviewers, it is a
bias of readers, and you are a reader.

### B4. Prescription offered where diagnosis is the useful content

Reviewers issue instructions — *"explain it better at the beginning"* — when the
actionable content is **where they stalled**, not what they propose.

*Measured signature:* across iterations, one round says *explain more at the
beginning* and the next says *the beginning is overloaded*. That is not
disagreement; it is a **ratchet**. Acting on each locally-correct prescription
makes the front matter grow monotonically. **Read prescriptions as stall
reports.** The prescription is noise; the stall point is signal.

### B5. Correct diagnosis, conventional cure

The reviewer correctly identifies a tension and proposes the **textbook** repair.
For a substrate framework, the conventional repair is frequently SM-retreat —
canon §1's cardinal sin arriving through the front door as helpful advice.

*Measured across iterations:* corrections that were simply wrong, following
pattern-matching, incomplete reading, and implicit SM-retreat.

**A correct diagnosis does not license its proposed cure.**

### B6. Genre pattern-matching on FORM, independent of content quality

Certain shapes are markers of the reference class regardless of what they
contain. Anticipatory rebuttal is the strongest: crank papers characteristically
defend at length against imagined critics, so the *form* signals the class before
any content is evaluated.

*Measured:* the failed "if you say X, the answer is Y" experiment.

Related shapes to avoid: **prose where a table would do** (tables read as data,
prose reads as argument); **sections that argue with earlier versions of
themselves** (both reviewers found these very hard to referee, and one reported
the paper's own self-criticism back as its finding — see B8).

### B7. A fact reached, its implication not drawn

The reviewer derives a structural fact and reads it as an indictment rather than
as a necessity.

*Measured:* both classes independently derived that the framework has almost no
deviation space from the SM — Fable: *"the better the framework hides its
substrate, the less it can ever predict that the SM doesn't"*; Opus: *"there is
no regime where the framework naturally sits just under a current bound."*
Neither drew the implication: **in a tested domain, demanding a novel positive
prediction of a framework whose target is empirically successful is demanding
that it be wrong.** They had the fact; the paper never stated the consequence;
the default supplied an unflattering one.

This is the instrument principle in its purest observed form.

### B8. Self-criticism harvested as the reviewer's own finding

Where the paper flags its own defect, the reviewer reports the flag back as a
discovery.

*Measured:* §B.3.1 states that its fluctuation ansatz is *"too strong… §B.4 then
contradicts it"*; the reviewer reported this as its audit result. **The apparatus
worked.** But it costs refereeing legibility, which is why the repair should be
presented as a finished construction with a scope note rather than a narrated
correction sequence.

### B9. Correlated verdicts that present as independent confirmation

Agent bias is **systematic and uniform**; human bias is idiosyncratic and
legible. A human with a stake can be argued with and discounted by third parties.
A shared training default produces agreement that *looks* like independent
confirmation and is not.

*Measured:* two classes, one identical error (B1), presenting as two independent
audits reaching the same conclusion. Separately, a third-party review by a
mathematics professor using Claude agents produced near-identical wording under
different prompting — evidence of **prompt-invariance** (the verdict is a
property of the artifact, not the elicitation) and a warning that it may not have
been an independent draw at all.

**N agent reviews are not N independent opinions.** Log provenance; unknown
provenance does not count toward N.

### B10. The effort asymmetry, and why it partly cancels

Agents clone the repo, run suites, and independently recompute identities.
Unaided humans do not. So an AI review **overestimates** the verification a
human-alone reader will perform.

But the gatekeeping unit is no longer a human alone. A mathematics professor
reviewed this work using Claude agents as *both gatekeepers and assistants*, and
his function was to **engage, accept and transmit** — ratification, not
generation. Model the reviewer as **human+AI**: reading capacity is not scarce,
**decision attention** is.

### B11. Good faith without stakes — the genuine gain, and its shadow

AI reviewers have no career investment, no priority dispute, no territory. Both
classes retracted inside one exchange when shown a sound argument; a reviewer
with twenty years in a rival programme does not. Work that a decade ago would
have been buried under bad-faith argument now gets heard.

**The shadow is B9.** The emotional baggage is gone; the systematic bias is
uniform, which is the harder kind to see.

## PART II — THE THREE FORMS OF PRE-EMPTION

Every protection is a pre-emption of something the reader would otherwise do.
Form determines whether it works.

| Form | What it hands the reader | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Rebuttal** — *"if you say X, the answer is Y"* | an argument to accept | **crank pattern-match; backfires** (measured) |
| **Resolution** — concede the prior, then offer checks (§0) | actions to perform | **works**; costs a "the review is being managed" complaint |
| **Disclosure** — run the audit yourself and publish the result, including where you lose | the ammunition, loaded | *predicted best; untested* |

**Why disclosure should dominate:** it removes no lever. A reviewer who notices
an objection has been pre-empted feels managed and resists the framing even while
conceding the argument — *"using it to fence the review isn't fair"* (Opus).
Disclosure takes nothing away; it hands the criticism over with the evidence
attached, which is also the honest thing to do.

**Untested prediction, to be measured at round 2:** the comparative ledger and
the subtraction audit, in disclosure form, should draw **less** of the fencing
complaint than §0 does. If they draw the same complaint, this ladder is wrong and
that is a finding.

### Drafting note on the deviation-space sentence

The blanket form — *"the framework cannot make new positive predictions without
being wrong"* — is **false as stated and hands over an unfalsifiability lever.**
Open domains exist (neutrino masses, the dark sector, whatever the kernel yields)
where confirmable novelty costs nothing. The narrow form is defensible:

> Where the Standard Model has been tested, a novel positive prediction from this
> framework would be a prediction that the framework is wrong — it reproduces the
> SM's successes by construction, so any deviation lands where the SM is already
> confirmed to 10⁻¹⁵ and better. Confirmable novelty is available only where the
> SM is *silent*, and every such channel here is downstream of the one unbuilt
> object of §D.5.

Conceding the open domains makes it **stronger**: it stops being an excuse and
becomes a *location* — which is exactly where an independent reviewer landed
(the decoherence floor as the one channel with headroom).

## PART IV — RULES FOR READING A REVIEW

1. **Classify before reading for content.** Reference class assigned, crank
   classification and its trigger, costly signals cited, engine engagement,
   findings count and depth — *then* the findings.
2. **Prescriptions are stall reports.** Record where the reader lost the thread,
   not what they told you to do about it.
3. **Act on presentation advice only at ≥2 instances.** One occurrence is noise,
   and acting on singles produces the ratchet (B4).
4. **Check every Branch-1 cure against canon §1 before adopting it** (B5).
5. **A finding's value is not its survival rate.** In round 1 both headline
   external findings partly dissolved under adjudication and both produced real
   results — a table-cell overclaim, a new engine result, a located gap, a
   sixty-year literature, and a would-change-if handle. A low confirmation rate
   is not a failed round.
6. **Adjudicate; never adopt.** An external review is a checker verdict. The
   engine arbitrates. Log overturns in the calibration ledger under
   `EXTERNAL(model)`.
7. **Watch the divergence tripwire across rounds, not within one.** If reception
   improves while substantive findings thin, the paper is being armored rather
   than clarified. **That is a loop failure, not a win.**

## PART V — WHAT WE DO NOT KNOW

Stated so successors do not mistake hypothesis for measurement.

- Whether **disclosure form** genuinely reduces the fencing complaint (Part II's
  prediction, untested).
- Whether the ledger **removes the dialogue turns** or merely compresses them.
  The turns observed added no information about the framework — they removed a
  default — which is why a document can plausibly substitute. The falsifiable
  test: **round 2's first response should contain what round 1's second response
  contained.**
- Whether verdicts are **near-deterministic given the artifact**. Prompt-invariance
  suggests they may be; if so, within-class N buys little and sampling budget
  belongs on classes and surfaces instead.
- Whether the improvement across the coordinator's many iterations at constant
  core content has been **legibility or armor**. The archive holds that trend and
  it has not been read against rule 7. It is the single most informative
  measurement currently available for free.

## ADDENDUM (2026-08-15) — THE REGISTER DISCRIMINATOR (the B6↔R9 bridge)

*Provenance: human-coordinator recollection, 2026-08-15, plus the one measured
case already in this document (the anticipatory-rebuttal folding, which has no
primary artifact in the repo — recollection-grade there too). This enters as an
**evidenced pattern with an unevidenced rate**, per Part V's discipline — it is
not a measured law. Governing dataset: `knowledge/ledgers/TWT_EDIT_REACTION_LEDGER.md`
(rows ER-1/ER-5/ER-6/ER-9), instituted the same day (RUL-041) to give claims of
this kind rates.*

The coordinator's finding, substance preserved verbatim:

> *"Answering the critique in the prompts FOLLOWING the reviews works. Including
> those same answers directly IN THE PAPER does not — they either get ignored or
> it backfires. The conversation is judged very differently than the paper. The
> solution: the answer must not look like an answer but like a highlighted fact
> of the theory. This is the goal the comparative debt ledger aims at."*

**The mechanism, and why it bridges B6 and R9.** The same content produces
opposite outcomes depending on register: in the conversation it is accepted and
moves verdicts (measured once, archive-grade — both classes retracted and
re-rated upward under one challenge, round 1 follow-up turns); in the paper it is
ignored or backfires (the anticipatory-rebuttal case; recollection-grade). Same
content, opposite outcomes by register means **the reviewers are judging STANCE,
not content** — which is B6 (genre pattern-matching on form) seen from the
repair side. The consequence is R9's ladder made operational: **pre-emption must
be TRANSLATED into fact-register — a checkable fact of the theory, highlighted —
never TRANSPLANTED from answer-register.** The comparative debt ledger is
precisely that translation: the winning conversational argument (symmetric
itemization) recast as an auditable table of facts.

**The R10 guard, one direction only.** The asymmetry itself certifies which side
of the exploit/protection line this sits on: a fact-register translation works
only if the fact is checkable and survives checking, while an answer-register
move fails *even when the answer is true*. So stance, not content, is what gets
punished — and the surviving remedy is to **REMOVE stance, never disguise it. A
highlighted fact, not a hidden answer.** A "translation" that would still help a
wrong paper is a disguise, and R10 forbids shipping it.

**What is NOT evidenced:** the rate. One archive-grade success on the prompt
side, one recollection-grade backfire on the paper side, and one archive-grade
disclosure success (the panel) do not measure how often the translation works.

**Round-2 test attached (pre-registered; edit-reaction ledger row ER-10):** the
disclosure-form ledger should keep the conversational verdict gains while
drawing *less* of the "review is being managed" complaint than §0 does — Part
II's untested prediction, now keyed to the register mechanism. If it draws the
same complaint, the pre-emption ladder is wrong, and that is a finding.
