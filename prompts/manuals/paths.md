# MANUAL · THE PATHS LEDGER — read this before you choose a route, or judge someone's choice

**Trigger: you are about to take one route and leave others; you are reviewing work that took a
route; or a route has just DIED and the ledger must be re-ranked.**
Complete for the activity: read this and you need nothing else to keep the ledger correctly.

> **The instrument, in one sentence (human coordinator, 2026-08-27):** *a ledger of the paths
> not taken and why — maintained by workers AND reviewers, read from time to time to make sure
> that none of those choices has changed from a secondary path to a best path.*

---

## 1 · WHY IT EXISTS — the asymmetry nothing else in the apparatus covers

**A wrong claim is caught by six instruments. A path never taken is caught by none.**

Everything else here guards the record against asserting too much: the tier system, the §8a
triad, the registries, the gates. Nothing guards against the programme quietly narrowing —
against the route that was noticed, judged second-best in about four seconds, and then never
thought about again, including on the day the first-choice route died.

The negatives ledger is its sibling and covers a different object:

| ledger | records | the entry's shape |
|---|---|---|
| **NEGATIVES** | what was **tried and failed** | tried X → failed because Y → would change if Z |
| **PATHS** | what was **seen and not tried** | considered X → not taken because Y → **rank R** → becomes first choice if Z |

**The distinction is load-bearing.** A negative is evidence: something was attempted and the
world answered. A path-not-taken is a *judgment*, made cheaply, usually under time pressure,
often by an instance that has since been dismissed — and judgments decay in a way evidence does
not. The ledger exists because **the fork's ranking was correct when it was made and nobody is
scheduled to notice when it stops being correct.**

---

## 2 · THE ENTRY

One entry per path, grouped under its **FORK** — the question the paths are alternative answers
to. A path outside a fork is an orphan and cannot be re-ranked, so name the fork first.

```markdown
## FORK P-07 — <the question these paths are alternative answers to>
Opened <date> · taken: P-07b · last re-ranked <date> (<why: a death / a consolidation / a landing>)

| id | the path, in one line | rank | grade | not taken because | becomes first choice if |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-07a | ... | 2 | B | ... | ... |
| P-07b | ... | 1 TAKEN | A | — | — |
| P-07c | ... | 3 | C | ... | ... |
```

**Every column is load-bearing:**

- **rank** — the ordering *within this fork*, and it is **relative, never absolute**. Rank 2 does
  not mean "poor"; it means "second, given what we knew on the day". This is the column that
  moves.
- **grade — TRACTABILITY, not promise.** Reuses the founding programme's own worklist scale,
  because tractability is a statement about the *work*, which you can assess, rather than about
  the *answer*, which you cannot:

  | grade | means |
  |---|---|
  | **A** | computation remaining — the route is specified; what is left is work you know how to do |
  | **B** | mechanism asserted — you know what to build, not that it can be built |
  | **C** | needs a new idea — no known route in |
  | **D** | blocked on a NAMED object — tractable in principle, waiting on something unbuilt or unmeasured **elsewhere**. *Name the object.* This is the grade that flips fastest, because someone else's landing can change it without anyone touching this fork. |

- **not taken because** — the actual reason, in the author's words. *"Less promising"* is not a
  reason; it is a rank with no argument. Acceptable reasons name a cost, a dependency, a
  conflict with something banked, or an honest **"we had one session and picked one"**.
- **becomes first choice if** — the promotion condition, and it is this ledger's would-change-if.
  **Write it so that someone else could notice it had been met.** A condition only its author
  could evaluate is a condition nobody will evaluate.

## 2-bis · ★ HOW TO GRADE — the procedure, because a grade assigned by feel is a rank with no argument

The grade is the ledger's load-bearing column: it is what a re-rank moves, and what a coordinator
reads when a path above it dies. **A grade is a claim about the WORK, and it carries a claim's
burden.**

**Grade by asking what is MISSING, in this order. Stop at the first that applies.**

1. **Is the route specified, with only execution left?** → **A**. You can name the steps. The
   honest test: *could you write the brief for it right now?* If yes it is A, however long the
   execution is. Length is not intractability.
2. **Do you know what to build, but not that it can be built?** → **B**. A mechanism is asserted
   and its construction is the open part.
3. **Is it waiting on something unbuilt or unmeasured ELSEWHERE?** → **D**, and **name the
   object.** D is not a weaker C: it is a *different shape*, and it is **the grade that flips
   fastest**, because someone else's landing moves it without anyone touching this fork. An
   unnamed D is a C wearing a hopeful label.
4. **Only if none of the above** → **C**, needs a new idea.

**★ THE FAILURE MODE THIS PROCEDURE EXISTS AGAINST, and it is the common one: grading C when the
truth is "I did not look outside my own branch."** C is the grade an agent reaches for when a
route is unfamiliar rather than blocked — and it is self-confirming, because a C is not worked,
so nothing ever contradicts it. Before writing C, discharge **C-35** in one line:

> **who looked, from which branch, with what tools** — and if the answer is *"the literature
> appears silent"*, that is **not a C.** Silence is a fact about people, not about the problem
> (C-35), and your range across branches is precisely what the literature's specialists did not
> have (C-34). **A route nobody tried from your angle is UNTRIED, and untried is not refuted.**

**So a C carries its search, or it is not a C.** Name the branches you actually looked in. If you
looked in one, the honest grade is **C-unsearched** — and a C-unsearched is a *cheap docket item*
(go look), not a wall.

**Two more disciplines that keep the column honest:**

- **Grade the ROUTE, never the ANSWER.** *"This probably will not work"* is a forecast and belongs
  in `[FORECAST]`; *"this needs a mechanism nobody has built"* is a grade. Confusing the two puts
  your credence about the world into a column that is supposed to describe the work — and a
  pessimistic credence then reads as a structural obstruction to everyone downstream.
- **Re-grade on the trigger, not on mood.** A grade moves when its *missing thing* changes: an
  object lands (D→B or A), a construction succeeds (B→A), a search is finally run (C-unsearched
  → whatever it actually is). Nothing else moves a grade — least of all how the fork feels after
  a bad week.

**Why the procedure is worth its cost.** Ranking is what makes a paths ledger an instrument
rather than a list: the death trigger promotes *the next best live path*, and "next best" is only
meaningful if the grades were assigned by a rule. **A ledger of feelings re-ranked by feeling
tells you what the programme was in the mood for, in the order it happened.**

## 3 · WHO WRITES — workers AND reviewers, and the reviewer's half is the one that bites

**THE WORKER** records the fork when it takes a route. Its brief asks for this on return
(`[ADJACENT]` — `coordinator_agent.md`, brief format). One fork, three lines, at the moment the
alternatives are still in view — which is the *only* moment they are cheap to write down.

**THE REVIEWER (and any checking role)** records what the worker did **not** say. This is a
genuine finding class and it belongs beside the existing ones:

> **UNSTATED-FORK** — the work took a route; a real alternative existed; the record does not say
> it was considered, or says so without a reason. Report it, and add the fork.

It is the exact sibling of **UNDER-CLAIM**: both are the corpus asserting less than it should —
UNDER-CLAIM about what a result earned, UNSTATED-FORK about what a choice cost. And a reviewer
is placed to see it precisely because it is *not* the author: the author's unconsidered
alternative is invisible to the author by construction.

**THE PHILOSOPHER** feeds it natively. Its interrogation programme's third outcome is *"not
forced — a genuine menu-pick; enumerating the menu is itself a result"* — **that enumeration is
a fork**, and it belongs here rather than only in a prose note.

## 4 · ★ WHEN IT IS READ — the trigger that makes it an instrument rather than a graveyard

A ledger written and never re-read is a tombstone field. Three reads, and the first is the one
that matters:

**(i) THE DEATH TRIGGER — SAME PASS, MANDATORY.** **Whenever a path DIES — a negative is banked,
a route is refuted, a dependency is lost — the fork it belonged to is RE-RANKED IN THE SAME
PASS.** Not later, not at consolidation: in the pass that records the death. The bank is where
this bites, and `manuals/banking.md` carries it as a banking duty.

*This is the whole mechanic the ledger exists for:* **a rank that was not good enough can become
the best choice the moment the path above it dies** — and nobody will notice unless the death
itself forces the look. The failure mode without it is precise and common: the first-choice
route dies, the programme records an honest negative, and moves on to *a new idea* while the
second-best route from the same fork sits in a file nobody opened.

**(ii) AT SESSION START — the coordinator.** Formation includes reading the ledger's LIVE
entries and asking of each: *has anything landed since that meets this promotion condition?*
This is the same act the consolidation ritual's step 0 performs on the negatives ledger's
would-change-ifs, and it sits at session start for the same reason: that is the moment the
docket is chosen.

**(iii) AT EVERY DEBT MODIFICATION — the philosopher.** A promotion condition is usually a
premise question, and this role is the one saturated with the alternatives.

> **Why NOT the meta-observer** *(considered and refused)*: its diet is per-claim and starved,
> and re-ranking a corpus-wide docket structure is a coordinator-shaped act. Handing it this
> duty would merge two roles, and **the merge destroys a measurement, not merely tidiness.**

## 5 · STATUS, AND HOW AN ENTRY LEAVES

| status | means |
|---|---|
| **LIVE** | still a real alternative; carries a rank and a promotion condition |
| **PROMOTED** | became the taken path — say what promoted it and when |
| **TRIED** | someone worked it; the outcome now lives in the negatives ledger (if it failed) or in the record (if it did not). **Leave the row, stamped, pointing there** |
| **CLOSED** | no longer an alternative because the fork's question dissolved — name what dissolved it |

**Retract by replacement, never by deletion** (the standing rule): a path that stops being a
path keeps its row, stamped. The trail is the dataset — *how often does a second-choice route
get promoted?* is a question about the programme's own judgment, and it is answerable only if
the rows survive.

---

## 6 · THE TWO FAILURE MODES OF THIS INSTRUMENT ITSELF

**It becomes a graveyard.** Entries accumulate, nothing is ever promoted, and the ledger reads
as diligence while functioning as landfill. **The detector is mechanical and it is in the
telemetry** (`honesty_telemetry.py`, signal 5): if negatives are being banked while the paths
ledger has not moved, **the death trigger is not firing** — and a promotion count of zero after
many entries means the ranking is decorative.

**It becomes scope creep.** Every dispatch returns a fork, forks breed, and the docket drowns in
alternatives nobody will ever work. Three guards: **one fork per dispatch** unless the work
genuinely opened several; **a path with no promotion condition is not recorded** (if you cannot
say what would make it first choice, you have not identified a path — you have had a feeling);
and the coordinator triages promotions into the docket like any other item, at the human
coordinator's recorded priority. **The ledger proposes; it never schedules.**
