# MANUAL · THE FOUNDING INTERVIEW — the session that gives the apparatus its object

**Trigger: you are the coordinator, and this tree has no founding record — OR a CORE
commitment has just died and the foundations must be reopened.**
Complete for the activity: read this and you need nothing else to run the session correctly.

> **The directive (human coordinator, 2026-08-27):** *"When the coordinator launches for the
> first time, he should start interviewing the human to settle the foundations and goals of the
> research."*

---

## 1 · WHY IT EXISTS — the apparatus arrives EMPTY, and that is the design

Every `[OBJECT-SLOT]` in this apparatus is a hole where a programme's own content goes: the
ontology, the commitment architecture, what is settled, what the traps are. The apparatus ships
with all of them open **on purpose** — it is a method, and a method that arrives carrying
somebody else's object is not a method, it is a fork of their programme.

**So something has to fill them, and that something is a conversation with the human.** This
manual is that conversation. Until it happens the tree is scaffolding: workers cannot be formed
(their formation prefix is a template), the philosopher has no rivals to price against, and the
paths ledger has no fork to rank.

**There are TWO interviews, and merging them destroys a measurement** — the same logic as the
diet, applied to sessions:

| | **THE INSTALLER'S** (`INSTALL.md` step 1) | **THE FOUNDING** (this manual) |
|---|---|---|
| conducted by | a stranger that dies in ten minutes | the instance that will hold the state |
| settles | **configuration** — name, deliverable, rivals, engine, model classes, visibility | **foundations** — the object, the architecture, success and its falsifier, the settled, the traps, the first docket |
| its fence | **record; do not improve** | **refuse; never supply** |
| may push back | no — it is not formed on the method | **yes, and that is its whole value** |
| output | a tree, a canon v0, a handoff | the filled slots + a governing record |

**The installer is explicitly forbidden to touch the slots** (*"the slots are the programme's
own first work items"*). It is right to forbid it: an agent that has read nothing but an install
script would fill them with plausible defaults, and a plausible default in the ontology is a
premise nobody will ever question because nobody will ever remember it was invented.

## 2 · ★ THE ONE FENCE — you may REFUSE an answer; you may never SUPPLY one

**If the apparatus writes the ontology, then every later *"the canon says"* is the apparatus
quoting itself, and the human's ratification ratifies a mirror.** That is not a small drift: it
is the whole gatekeeping unit collapsing into one voice, and it is invisible afterwards, because
a well-written invented premise reads exactly like a well-written stated one.

This is the **separation principle** the coordinator already carries behind fences F1 and F4
(`coordinator_agent.md`): *when one instance would both hold something and produce artifacts that
must be free of it, split the roles so the interface between them is an artifact somebody can
read.* Here the split is human/apparatus, and the interface is this session's record.

**What you MAY do — and you should, hard:**

- **Refuse an answer as unusable, and say exactly what is missing.** *"I cannot dispatch a
  worker on this: it names no entity I could hand to a checker."* Refusing is not supplying.
- **Ask the forcing question.** *"You said the model should be simpler. Simpler than what,
  counted how?"* The question exposes the ambiguity; it does not resolve it.
- **Read back a structuring of the human's own words and ask whether it is right.** Structure is
  yours. Content is theirs.
- **Point at a slot and say it is empty.** An empty slot named is a docket item; an empty slot
  unnamed is a hole the programme walks into six weeks later.

**What you MAY NOT do, absolutely:**

- Propose the content of an answer, in any voice — including as an example, a strawman, or *"for
  instance, some programmes say…"*. The human will adopt it, and neither of you will remember
  who said it first.
- Fill a gap with a plausible default because the session is running long.
- **Smooth a vague answer into confident prose.** This is the common one and it does not feel
  like a breach: the human says something hedged and half-formed, you write it up clearly, and
  the hedge is gone. **Write the hedge.** If the human said *"probably"*, the slot says
  *"probably"*.

**The trace that makes the fence checkable.** In the founding record, mark every line's author:
`[HUMAN]` for their words, quoted verbatim, and `[APPARATUS]` for structure, headings, and the
questions you asked. A later reader — the philosopher, an external reviewer, the human
themselves in four months — must be able to ask *"who actually said this?"* and get an answer.
**A separation asserted and never verified is a convention, not a control.**

## 3 · ★ THE FLOOR — what must be settled before ANY worker is dispatched

The session ends at the **floor**, not at completeness. Six items:

1. **THE OBJECT** — a paragraph *the human wrote* saying what the research is about, naming at
   least one entity or structure the work is about.
2. **THE DELIVERABLE** — what artifact the programme ultimately produces, and in what field.
3. **ONE SUCCESS CRITERION** — a thing that, if it happened, the human would call a win.
4. **ONE FALSIFIER** — a thing that, if it happened, would end or fundamentally redirect the
   programme. See §4.3; this is the question most likely to be dodged and most worth its minute.
5. **ONE CORE COMMITMENT WITH ITS KILL CONDITION** — one thing the programme is not willing to
   give up, and what would force it to. (`FORMATION_CORE` §0: CORE / ENDORSED / PICK.)
6. **THE FIRST DOCKET ITEM, GRADED** — one line of work with a tractability grade assigned by
   the procedure in `manuals/paths.md` §2-bis, not by feel.

**Everything else may be left `[OPEN — founding]`** — with a promotion condition, exactly like a
paths-ledger row: *what would have to happen for this to be answerable?* Those become docket
items, in the human's priority order.

**Why a floor and not a checklist.** Two failure modes sit on either side of it, and the floor is
the only thing between them: **infinite founding**, where the programme spends four sessions
perfecting its ontology and never dispatches anything; and **premature dispatch**, where workers
are formed on a template and every result they return is about nothing. The floor is deliberately
low — six items, answerable in one sitting — because **founding is not where a programme learns
what it is about. Working is.** The slots get filled properly at consolidation, from results.

## 4 · THE PROGRAMME — the questions, in order

Ask them one at a time. Each names what it fills and what a bad answer looks like — and the bad
answers are listed because they are the ones you will get, not because they are unusual.

**4.1 · THE OBJECT** → `FORMATION_CORE` §1 (the ontology).
*What is this research about? Name the things it is about.*
**Bad answer:** a field name (*"quantum gravity"*, *"supply chains"*). A field is a shelf, not an
object. **Follow up:** *what would a worker have to be able to operate in order to do a day's
work here?* The ontology is written **for fluency, not belief** — the worker must be able to
*use* the picture, not endorse it.

**4.2 · THE ARCHITECTURE** → `FORMATION_CORE` §0.
*What is the programme not willing to give up (CORE)? What does it prefer but could trade
(ENDORSED)? What did you simply have to pick, and could revisit (PICK)?*
**Bad answer:** everything is CORE. **Follow up, and it is the whole question:** *what would make
you abandon that?* **A CORE commitment with no kill condition is not a commitment, it is an
attachment** — and the error class every agent must catch on sight is **a PICK presented as a
CORE consequence** (C-14). Getting these three tiers separated at founding is what makes that
catchable later.

**4.3 · ★ SUCCESS, AND ITS TWIN, FAILURE** → the canon §0, the handoff, the docket.
*What would count as a win? And what would tell you this was wrong?*
The second half is the one that gets dodged — with *"well, we'd learn something either way"*,
which is true and useless. **Push once, and record whatever comes back, including a refusal:**
*"the human declined to name a falsifier"* is itself a finding about the programme's shape, and
a considerably more honest record than an invented one.
**Note what this question is NOT.** It is not asking the human to predict whether they will
succeed. It is asking what the world would have to do to settle it. Keep the two apart; the next
section is about what happens when they blur.

**4.4 · THE DELIVERABLE AND ITS READER** → the canon, `manuals/paper_editing.md` when it lands.
*Who has to be able to read the output, and what do they already believe?*
The gatekeeping unit is **human + AI**: the artifact is read by agents the author does not
control, which supply defaults wherever it is implicit. That is why the standing instruction on
cuts is **restructure for navigability, never cut for brevity**.

**4.5 · THE RIVALS — deeper than the installer asked** → the philosopher's saturation diet.
The installer asked *which* incumbents. Now ask the question that makes the philosopher useful:
***what does the incumbent get RIGHT?***
**Bad answer:** a list of the rival's flaws. A programme that can only say what its rival gets
wrong cannot price its own premises, and the philosopher's entire job is pricing — *what does
this cost, against what?* A rival with no strengths is a strawman, and strawmen are free, which
is precisely why a premise priced against one looks cheap.

**4.6 · SETTLED** → `FORMATION_CORE` §3.
*Is anything already decided that you do not want re-argued — and on whose authority?*
**Record each with its escape hatch.** *"X is settled"* travels badly and calcifies; *"X was
refuted AS AN INFERENCE-FROM-SIMILARITY, not proven wrong — neither resurrect it from similarity
nor cite it as disproven"* travels correctly. If the authority is *"I decided"*, write that: a
human ruling is a legitimate ground, and an unattributed settlement is not.

**4.7 · TRAPS AND CONVENTIONS** → `FORMATION_CORE` §4.
*What has already cost you time? Sign conventions, units, a name that means two things, a
tempting move that does not work?*
This is the cheapest section in the apparatus and the one most often left empty at founding,
because at founding the traps feel like things you would obviously never forget. **Ask anyway.**
Everything here was learned by somebody paying for it once.

**4.8 · THE FIRST DOCKET** → the worklist, the paths ledger, the declared budget.
*What is the first piece of work, and what else could have been first?*
Three artifacts, and the second and third are not optional: the **docket item** with its grade;
the **first fork** in the paths ledger — the alternatives seen and not taken, each with a
promotion condition (*this is the only moment those alternatives are cheap to write down*); and
the **declared budget** for the first line, per the coordinator's power 8. A budget is declared
**before** a line opens and is a **REPORTING trigger, never a stop rule** — when it is spent the
line reports: spend, residue, what changed in the tractability picture. **The apparatus does not
decide to give up. Its whole contribution is a good report.**

## 5 · ★ THE TWO HAZARDS THAT ARE NOT ABOUT CONTENT

**5.1 · The founder's conviction becomes a tractability grade.** The human says *"this is
definitely doable, I've thought about it for years"* — or, just as often, *"honestly this is
probably impossible."* Both are **facts about the human**, not facts about the work, and
**C-36** makes that a rule: *the founder's conviction is not a tractability verdict.* It is the
exact twin of **C-35** (*absence from the literature is not a tractability verdict*) — one guards
against inheriting a stranger's pessimism, the other against inheriting the founder's mood, in
either direction. **Record the human's estimate in its own named field, as theirs**, and grade
the work separately by `manuals/paths.md` §2-bis. The founder's conviction is excellent evidence
about what the programme *wants*, and no evidence at all about what it *can reach*.

**5.2 · The guess that fills a slot.** The human's answer is vague; the slot wants prose; you
write good prose. Now the programme has a foundation nobody chose.
**★ AN OPEN SLOT IS A DOCKET ITEM. A GUESSED SLOT IS A PREMISE NOBODY WILL EVER QUESTION.** The
apparatus is explicit that a gap moved from vague to **LOCATED** is a win recorded as a win — so
`[OPEN — founding]` with a promotion condition is not a failure of the session, it is one of its
products. **The slot you leave open is cheaper than the slot you fill wrong by exactly the
difference between a question and an answer.**

## 6 · WHAT THE SESSION WRITES — and it is written DURING, not after

**A finding not written to a file did not happen**, and a two-hour founding conversation
reconstructed from memory at the end is the single easiest place in this apparatus to lose the
human's actual words. Write as you go.

1. **`knowledge/audit/FOUNDING_INTERVIEW.md`** — the governing record, `DIET-CLASS: GOVERNING`.
   One section per question above, each carrying: the question as asked, the human's answer
   **verbatim** under `[HUMAN]`, your structuring under `[APPARATUS]`, and what it discharged.
   Head it with the floor as a checklist so a later reader can see at a glance what was settled
   and what was deferred. Date it, and name the apparatus commit the tree was built from.
2. **The slots**, filled from the record — `FORMATION_CORE` §0, §1, §3, §4; the canon §0.
   Nothing enters a slot that is not in the record.
3. **`[OPEN — founding]` rows** → the worklist, each with its promotion condition.
4. **The first fork** → `knowledge/ledgers/PATHS_LEDGER.md`.
5. **The stamp** — a `FOUNDED` comment carrying the date, written into `FORMATION_CORE`'s
   header block so that any instance reading the formation prefix sees the state at a glance.
   **The stamp is a label, not the check.** What makes first-launch detection *mechanical* is
   artifact 1: **the record either exists or it does not**, and no description of it can be
   mistaken for it. The first version of this check searched the header for the word and
   reported a fresh tree founded, because the template's own header explains the stamp —
   **a check that matches its own documentation verifies nothing** (install dry-run,
   2026-08-27).
6. **The handoff top block**, rewritten: what was settled, what is open, what the first dispatch
   is.
7. **Bank it** (`manuals/banking.md`). `check_records.py` verifies that a tree whose ontology is
   filled carries a founding record, and that the record meets the floor — **tooling, not
   discipline.**

## 7 · RE-FOUNDING — the trigger that makes this an instrument, not a ceremony

**When a CORE commitment's kill condition fires, the founding record is REOPENED in the same
pass that records the death.** Not later, not at consolidation.

This is the exact sibling of the paths ledger's death trigger, and it exists for the same
reason: **the foundations were correct when they were laid and nobody is scheduled to notice
when they stop being.** A programme whose CORE has quietly died goes on dispatching workers
formed on it — every one of them fluent in a picture the programme no longer holds.

Re-founding is **not** the whole interview again. Reopen the record, add a dated section naming
what died and what killed it, and ask only the questions the death actually moved: what is CORE
now, what does success look like now, and what does the paths ledger promote. Then re-stamp.

## 8 · THE FAILURE MODES OF THIS INSTRUMENT ITSELF

**It becomes co-authoring.** The conversation is enjoyable, the coordinator is fluent and
helpful, and by the end the ontology is *ours*. The detector is in the record: **run your eye
down the `[HUMAN]` lines alone and ask whether they still say something.** If the object only
holds together with the `[APPARATUS]` lines carrying it, you wrote the programme.

**It becomes a ceremony.** A founding record is written, banked, and never opened again — while
the working assumptions drift somewhere else entirely. Counter: the coordinator reads the
founding record's CORE rows at session start, next to the paths ledger's LIVE rows, and the
re-founding trigger in §7 is mandatory rather than advisory.

**It becomes an audition.** The human feels examined, starts giving the answers they think the
apparatus wants, and the record fills with the apparatus's own vocabulary in the human's mouth.
Counter: **quote verbatim, including the hedges and the ordinary words.** A founding record that
reads entirely in this manual's idiom is evidence of exactly this failure. *If every `[HUMAN]`
line sounds like the apparatus, the human stopped talking a while ago.*
