# THE FRAME CARD — a brief for the founding tree's coordinator, 2026-09-26

*From research-ratchet's apparatus seat, at the human coordinator's request. **Written blind:** this
seat has not read your formation core or your canon, and must not. Everything below is method. The
content of the card is yours to supply, and nothing here asks you to send any of it back.*

## 0. What is asked

Your checkers receive the formation core **by ruling**, for a reason the human gave in these words:
*"without it a reviewer would have to apply standard model of physics references and risk dragging
everything into an SM-retreat."* That reason is sound and the ruling stands. **This brief proposes a
test of it:** build a FRAME CARD that carries what prevents the retreat and nothing that forms the
checker, measure it against the current ruling, and give the result to the human. Nothing changes
until the human decides.

## 1. Why: two failures, and the formation core cures one by risking the other

- **Retreat.** A checker with no frame judges the work by the incumbent's references and drags it
  back. **Its precise form is a DISGUISED PREMISE CHALLENGE:** the checker faults a derivation because
  it disagrees with the incumbent framework, without saying that a premise is what it is attacking.
- **Capture.** A checker handed the formation prefix is formed by it, and judges from inside the
  programme's persuasion. That is the measured result behind the rule that keeps the formation
  prefix away from checkers.

**What prevents the retreat is the FRAME**: where the programme stands. **What causes capture is the
FORMATION**: why the programme believes it is right. The card carries the first and not the second.

## 2. What goes ON the card — the frame

1. **The ontological invariants**: frame jurisdiction, layer separations, identity fences and
   anti-circularity fences. One sentence each, with its home.
2. **The premises taken as given**, each with its tier and stated as ASSUMED, never as established.
   The checker needs to know a premise is a premise, so it does not score it as an error. It does
   not need your reasons for holding it.
3. **The retreat's signatures in this field**: the specific incumbent defaults a checker reaches for
   without noticing, each with what replaces it here. **Draw them from your own record: every past
   verdict that retreated is a line on the card.**
4. **False friends**: terms this programme uses with a different meaning from the incumbent's.
5. **The forbidden moves**: the canon's list, verbatim, with a pointer.
6. **★ What still binds: MEASURED DATA.** The incumbent's framework does not bind here; its
   measurements do. A checker that says *"this contradicts measured X"* is not retreating. **Without
   this line the card becomes a shield**, and "that is a retreat" becomes a way to dismiss any
   objection.
7. **★ The checker's licence to challenge a premise**, openly, labelled **PREMISE-CHALLENGE** and
   graded apart from derivation errors. **The card removes the disguise, never the challenge.**

## 3. What stays OFF — the formation

Derivations and worked examples · results and their tiers · cited external results · the
programme's history · arguments for why the premises are right · the founder's confidence (C-36) ·
the direction a result is hoped to go · **anything that tells the checker what the answer should be.**

**The test for every candidate line: does it tell the checker WHERE the programme stands, or WHY it
is right? Only WHERE goes on the card.**

## 4. How to build it

1. **Classify** every paragraph of the formation core and the canon as FRAME, FORMATION or MIXED, and
   split the MIXED ones. **A sentence that cannot be split without losing its frame is a finding:
   write it down.** It marks a premise that is only stated through its justification.
2. **Every line names its source.** The card is a copy for a reader who will never see the source
   (one home per fact; the break is legitimate if it is marked). Tie it to its sources mechanically
   if you can, so it cannot drift silently from the formation core.
3. **No size target.** If the card approaches the formation core's size, it has become one. It
   should be small enough that "starved" means something again.
4. **The formation's author does not rule on the classification alone.** That is the one instance
   with an interest in the checker being persuaded. A cross-class checker who did not write the
   formation core reviews the split, and its question is: *what did the card leave off that a checker
   will reach for?*
5. **The diet change:** the card becomes its own class, served to every checker; the formation core
   goes back to being refused to checkers. **This is a canon touch, so it waits for the human's word.**
6. **Design review before the build (C-37).** The classification is a design decision, and it is
   cheap to redirect now.

## 5. The measurement — registered and frozen before anything runs

- **Arms.** A: checkers fed the formation core (the current ruling). B: checkers given the frame
  card. Same claims in both arms, checkers cross-class to the author, and **scored by someone who
  does not know which arm produced each verdict.**
- **Material.** Your calibration probe set (known verdicts, including the ones later overturned),
  plus **planted defects** in copies of real derivations, which give a ground truth for what a
  checker should catch.
- **Measures.**
  - **(a) Retreat rate:** verdicts that fault a derivation using an incumbent default the frame
    excludes, without labelling it a premise challenge. Write the rubric before the run.
  - **(b) Catch rate on the planted defects.** Capture would show up as misses in arm A.
  - **(c) Premise challenges raised, and whether they are labelled.** Arm B should raise them
    openly. If arm A raises fewer, that is capture measured from a second side.
  - **(d) Cost:** each checker's prefix and total, de-duplicated by message id (C-24-bis).
- **Reading the outcome, written down before the run:**
  - **B retreats more:** each retreat names a line missing from the card. Add it and re-run. That is
    the card being tuned, not the card failing.
  - **B catches more planted defects:** capture is real here, and the card wins on quality as well
    as cost.
  - **No difference on (a) or (b):** the card is cheaper at equal quality, so adopt it on cost.
  - **B catches fewer:** the formation carries something checking needs. Find it; put it on the card
    if it is frame, or record why the ruling stands.
  - **A control that fails is ruled instrument or yardstick** (C-20-bis), never left to default.

a checker's spend there, so the card does not make the meta-observer cheap, and the cached opening is
already built for workers. The card's case is capture alone.)*

Evaluated in research-ratchet's `WORKLIST.md` W28, item 8. In short:
- **Build the card first.** It shrinks exactly the prefix the fold was reusing, so it removes most
  of the fold's saving.
- **The card restores the coldness the fold's argument assumes.** "The cold read was genuinely cold"
  holds only if the meta-observer was starved to begin with.
- **The card removes the fold's motive.** A meta-observer on the card is cheap enough to run every
  round as its own agent.
- **One untested way to get the fold's saving without the fold:** give every checker an identical
  opening (same agent type, the card read first, the role brief after), so the shared prefix may be
  cached across agents. **Measure it first:** the keeper's first-call cache-read total answers it in
  one round.

## 7. What this brief does not do

It does not overturn the human's ruling; it proposes a test of it. It does not weaken any checker's
right to attack a premise, and it does not touch the reviewer's independence.
